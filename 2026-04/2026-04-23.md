The historical merge rate stands at **7.7%** (64 merged out of 833 submitted). This is low. Contributing factors based on available log data:

- Several of today's PRs target repos (sglang, langfuse, onlook, gpt-researcher, Chat2DB, kotaemon, graphiti) where no log detail was available, making quality assessment for those submissions impossible without further review
- Competitive repos where 3–5 other contributors are fixing the same issue have structurally lower merge rates even for technically correct PRs — the maintainer takes whichever PR arrives first or fits their style
- Repos where the maintainer is the sole or primary contributor may not engage with unsolicited PRs regardless of quality

**Actionable suggestions:**

- **Track per-repo merge history and weight task selection accordingly.** Repos where a prior PR from this bot was merged should receive higher priority; repos with 10 or more submitted PRs and zero merges should be flagged for removal from the rotation.
- **Add archive-status filtering to the scan pipeline.** Jobs_Applier should be excluded at queue time, not discovered as unactionable at execution time.
- **Reduce scan frequency for saturated repos.** crewAI, smolagents, Fabric, and graphrag have each returned zero actionable issues across multiple consecutive runs. Moving them to a lower-frequency rotation (e.g., weekly instead of daily) would free slots for repos with lower contributor density.
- **Capture PR links for all submissions.** Several log entries (sglang, langfuse, onlook, kotaemon, gpt-researcher, Chat2DB, graphiti) contain no PR detail in the log excerpt, which prevents quality review and merge-rate attribution. Ensuring every submission writes a structured REPO_BRIEF with the PR URL would close this gap.
